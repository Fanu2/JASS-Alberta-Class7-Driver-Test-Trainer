# JASS Alberta Class 7 — 2026 Driver's Guide Exhaustive Study Q&A

**Source:** Government of Alberta *Driver's Guide — cars and light trucks*, Spring 2026.

This bank is designed as a comprehensive study/quiz bank based on the uploaded 2026 booklet. It paraphrases the booklet rather than reproducing it verbatim. The booklet states that the Class 7 knowledge test is based on questions taken from this guide and covers safe driving practices, driving laws and road signs.

**Total questions: 417**

## How to use this bank
- Study by chapter first.
- Then use the questions as flashcards.
- Pay special attention to numbers, distances, times, demerit points, signs and exceptions.
- The booklet itself says it is a guide and that drivers must use judgement and a safety-first attitude in real situations.

## 1. Licensing Information (pp. 10–20)

**Q1. What are the two stages of Alberta's GDL program for new drivers?**
**Answer:** Stage 1 is Class 7 learner; Stage 2 is Class 5-GDL probationary.

**Q2. At what age can a person apply for a Class 7 learner's licence?**
**Answer:** 14 years of age or older.

**Q3. What must an applicant pass before obtaining a Class 7 learner's licence?**
**Answer:** A vision screening and a knowledge test on the rules of the road; valid identification is also required, and parental consent is required if under 18.

**Q4. How long must a Class 7 learner hold the licence?**
**Answer:** At least one year.

**Q5. Who must accompany a Class 7 learner while driving?**
**Answer:** A fully licensed non-GDL driver who is at least 18 and seated next to the learner.

**Q6. When may a Class 7 learner not drive?**
**Answer:** Between midnight and 5 a.m.

**Q7. How many passengers may a Class 7 learner carry?**
**Answer:** No more passengers than there are seat belts.

**Q8. At how many demerit points is a Class 7 learner suspended?**
**Answer:** Eight or more.

**Q9. What alcohol/drug condition applies to Class 7 learners?**
**Answer:** Zero alcohol and/or drug level while driving.

**Q10. What is required to become a Class 5-GDL probationary driver?**
**Answer:** Hold Class 7 for one year, be at least 16, and pass the Alberta Class 5 road test.

**Q11. How long is the minimum probationary period for Class 5-GDL?**
**Answer:** Two years.

**Q12. What passenger restriction applies to Class 5-GDL?**
**Answer:** No more passengers than seat belts.

**Q13. At how many demerit points is a Class 5-GDL driver suspended?**
**Answer:** Eight or more.

**Q14. What alcohol/drug condition applies to Class 5-GDL?**
**Answer:** Zero alcohol and/or drug level while driving.

**Q15. Can a Class 5-GDL driver serve as a supervising driver for a learner?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q16. Can a Class 5-GDL driver re-class to Classes 1–4?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q17. What are the basic requirements to exit GDL according to the booklet?**
**Answer:** Be at least 18, be in the probationary stage for at least two years, and be free of suspensions or traffic violations during the last 12 months of the probationary stage, including zero alcohol/drug level while driving.

**Q18. By how much can the probationary stage be reduced through approved training?**
**Answer:** Up to six months, if the approved course results in condition code U and all other eligibility requirements are met.

**Q19. What changes when a driver exits GDL?**
**Answer:** The licence becomes full Class 5 non-GDL; the driver may re-class to Classes 1–4, has a higher demerit allowance, no longer has zero alcohol/drug tolerance, and may supervise a learner.

**Q20. What is the Class 7 knowledge test based on?**
**Answer:** Questions taken from the Driver's Guide, covering safe driving practices, driving laws, and road signs.

**Q21. How is the Class 7 knowledge test administered?**
**Answer:** On a computer as multiple-choice questions.

**Q22. How many times per day may the knowledge test be taken?**
**Answer:** Once per day.

**Q23. Is there a fee for each knowledge test?**
**Answer:** Yes.

**Q24. Which languages include Hindi and Punjabi for the Class 7 electronic test?**
**Answer:** Both Hindi and Punjabi are among the 25 listed languages.

**Q25. What is required before obtaining an Alberta driver's licence regarding vision?**
**Answer:** A vision assessment.

**Q26. What does Class 5 allow a driver to operate?**
**Answer:** Cars and other light vehicles within the Class 5 vehicle limits.

**Q27. What is the approximate length of the Class 5 road test?**
**Answer:** About 45 minutes.

**Q28. What must a road-test vehicle have?**
**Answer:** Valid plate and insurance; functioning lights, horn, speedometer, brakes/park brake, unobstructed windshield/wipers, good tires, doors, seats/seat belts, exhaust/muffler, mirrors, and enough fuel.

**Q29. What skills are assessed on the Class 5 road test?**
**Answer:** Vehicle handling, lane selection, speed zones, right-of-way, controlled and uncontrolled intersections, interaction with other road users, and parking.

**Q30. Which parking manoeuvres are listed for the Class 5 road test?**
**Answer:** Perpendicular, uphill, downhill, and parallel parking; drivers over 65 are not required to complete a parallel park.

**Q31. What can automatically fail a road test regardless of point totals?**
**Answer:** Examples include exceeding the speed limit or driving too fast for conditions; failing to stop completely at a stop sign or before a right turn on red; failing to yield; obstructing traffic by driving too slowly or stopping unnecessarily; climbing a curb while parking or being unable to park legally in three attempts; and failing to slow/observe adequately at uncontrolled intersections.

**Q32. What is required when applying for an Alberta driver's licence as a resident?**
**Answer:** Acceptable identification and appropriate residency/immigration documentation.

**Q33. Can a person hold more than one driver's licence?**
**Answer:** No; the booklet states it is illegal to hold more than one driver's licence.

**Q34. How long may a visitor with a valid out-of-province licence drive the same class in Alberta?**
**Answer:** Up to one year.

**Q35. How long may a new Alberta resident from another Canadian jurisdiction use the out-of-province licence?**
**Answer:** 90 days, after which an Alberta licence must be obtained and the old licence surrendered.

**Q36. Does Alberta have reciprocal licensing agreements with every jurisdiction?**
**Answer:** No. The booklet lists specific reciprocal jurisdictions.

**Q37. What can a person from a non-reciprocal jurisdiction apply for?**
**Answer:** Credit for previous driving history; a successful application can exempt the person from GDL and allow Class 5 road testing without first holding Class 7 for one year.

**Q38. What documents must be carried while driving?**
**Answer:** The driver's licence, insurance, and registration.

**Q39. Who conducts Class 4, 5 and 6 road tests?**
**Answer:** Licensed driver examiners through Alberta registry agents on behalf of the Government of Alberta.

**Q40. Are passengers or pets allowed on a road test?**
**Answer:** No, except the booklet's stated government-official training/monitoring exemption.

**Q41. What is an air brake endorsement called?**
**Answer:** Q.

**Q42. When is a Q air-brake endorsement required?**
**Answer:** For vehicles with an air-only braking system or a combination air/hydraulic braking system.

**Q43. What is the minimum age for Class 5-GDL?**
**Answer:** 16.

**Q44. What is the minimum age for Class 5 non-GDL?**
**Answer:** 18.

**Q45. What are examples of vehicles Class 5 may operate?**
**Answer:** A two-axle vehicle; certain recreational vehicles; certain vehicle-trailer combinations without air brakes; mopeds; and specified vehicles for learning with the required supervision.

**Q46. What may a Class 5 holder not operate as an ordinary Class 5 driver?**
**Answer:** A motorcycle unless learning, a vehicle seating more than 15 while transporting anyone other than the driver, a vehicle transporting passengers for hire, or an air-brake-equipped vehicle unless the supervisor has the required air-brake endorsement.

## 2. Traffic Control, Signals, Signs and Road Markings (pp. 22–40)

**Q1. What does a solid red traffic light require?**
**Answer:** A complete stop before the stop line or crosswalk; if neither exists, before the intersection.

**Q2. When may a driver turn right on a red light?**
**Answer:** Unless prohibited by a sign, after a complete stop and only when the turn is safe and other traffic is yielded to.

**Q3. What left turn is permitted on a red light?**
**Answer:** Unless prohibited, a left turn from a one-way street onto another one-way street, after a complete stop and yielding.

**Q4. What does a solid yellow light mean?**
**Answer:** Prepare to stop or clear the intersection; approaching drivers must stop unless they have reached a point where stopping cannot be done safely.

**Q5. What should a driver already in an intersection do when the light turns yellow?**
**Answer:** Safely clear the intersection.

**Q6. What does a solid green light permit?**
**Answer:** Proceed without stopping unless required to yield to oncoming traffic when turning left or to pedestrians in a crosswalk.

**Q7. What is a stale green light?**
**Answer:** A green light that has been green for a while and is likely to turn yellow soon.

**Q8. What pedestrian signal clue can help identify a stale green?**
**Answer:** A DON'T WALK indication means the driver should be ready for the traffic light to change.

**Q9. What does a green arrow with a solid green light permit?**
**Answer:** Proceed without yielding in the arrow's direction; other directions may also proceed when safe and legal.

**Q10. What does a green arrow with a red light permit?**
**Answer:** Proceed without stopping only in the direction indicated by the arrow.

**Q11. What does a flashing red light mean?**
**Answer:** Stop and then proceed only when safe after yielding; it is treated like a stop sign.

**Q12. What happens when signals in all directions are flashing red?**
**Answer:** The intersection becomes a four-way stop.

**Q13. What does a flashing yellow light mean?**
**Answer:** Proceed with caution after yielding to pedestrians and other vehicles within the intersection.

**Q14. What does a flashing green light permit?**
**Answer:** Proceed through, turn left, or turn right without stopping, while still yielding to pedestrians or other vehicles lawfully in the intersection.

**Q15. What does WALK mean for a pedestrian?**
**Answer:** The pedestrian may enter the crosswalk or roadway when safe.

**Q16. What does DON'T WALK/WAIT mean?**
**Answer:** A pedestrian must not enter; someone already crossing may continue and clear the crosswalk.

**Q17. What does a red X over a reversible lane mean?**
**Answer:** Do not enter or remain in that lane; move safely to a lane with a green arrow.

**Q18. What does a downward green arrow over a lane mean?**
**Answer:** Traffic is permitted to travel in that lane.

**Q19. What does a red circle with a slash generally indicate?**
**Answer:** The action or activity inside the circle is not permitted.

**Q20. What does a green circle on a white square indicate?**
**Answer:** The action shown is permitted.

**Q21. What does a fluorescent yellow-green pentagon indicate?**
**Answer:** A school zone or school area.

**Q22. What does a white message on a black square indicate?**
**Answer:** Lane control.

**Q23. What does an orange diamond sign generally indicate?**
**Answer:** Construction-area information.

**Q24. What does a green rectangle generally provide?**
**Answer:** Route, destination, distance, or other information/guide information.

**Q25. What does a blue square generally indicate?**
**Answer:** Fuel, food, lodging, assistance, or other service information depending on the symbol.

**Q26. What does a yellow diamond sign generally indicate?**
**Answer:** Caution or warning of a hazard.

**Q27. What does a stop sign require?**
**Answer:** A complete stop and no proceeding until safe.

**Q28. What does a yield sign require?**
**Answer:** Yield to traffic not facing the sign and to pedestrians waiting to cross or within the crosswalk/intersection.

**Q29. What does a WRONG WAY sign indicate?**
**Answer:** Traffic is moving in an oncoming direction.

**Q30. What does a DO NOT ENTER sign mean?**
**Answer:** Do not enter.

**Q31. What does a railway crossing sign require?**
**Answer:** Yield to trains and follow the crossing controls.

**Q32. What does a DO NOT STOP ON TRACKS sign mean?**
**Answer:** Do not stop on railway tracks.

**Q33. What is the maximum speed on a provincial highway outside an urban area unless otherwise posted?**
**Answer:** 100 km/h.

**Q34. What is the maximum speed on a provincial highway inside a city's corporate limits unless otherwise posted?**
**Answer:** 80 km/h.

**Q35. What is the maximum speed on a roadway outside an urban area unless otherwise posted?**
**Answer:** 80 km/h.

**Q36. What is the maximum speed on a roadway within an urban area unless otherwise posted?**
**Answer:** 50 km/h.

**Q37. Can a municipality set different speed limits?**
**Answer:** Yes; local bylaws may establish different maximum limits, so posted signs and local laws must be followed.

**Q38. Do speed-limit signs mean drivers should always travel at that speed?**
**Answer:** No. They are the maximum permitted when conditions are ideal; an unsafe speed for current conditions is illegal.

**Q39. What does a designated-lane sign tell a driver?**
**Answer:** The direction(s) a vehicle must travel in that lane.

**Q40. What is a reserved lane?**
**Answer:** A lane reserved for specified vehicles such as buses, taxis, or bicycles, sometimes only during posted times.

**Q41. What do permissive signs look like?**
**Answer:** A green circle on a white square.

**Q42. What do prohibitive signs show?**
**Answer:** A red circle and slash over the prohibited action/activity.

**Q43. When can a driver enter a bicycle lane?**
**Answer:** Vehicles generally must not drive in it, except when turning or entering/exiting a parking spot; the line may be dashed near corners to permit entry for a right turn.

**Q44. What do solid white lines mean?**
**Answer:** Lane changing is not permitted across them.

**Q45. What do broken white lines mean?**
**Answer:** Lane changing is permitted when safe.

**Q46. What do yellow lines mean?**
**Answer:** Traffic on opposite sides of the line travels in opposite directions; the yellow line should normally be on the driver's left.

**Q47. What do broken yellow lines mean?**
**Answer:** Passing is permitted when the lane is clear and the pass can be completed safely.

**Q48. What does a solid yellow line normally mean?**
**Answer:** Passing is not permitted, except the booklet states that passing safely is permitted across a single solid yellow line within an urban area.

**Q49. What does a solid-and-broken yellow combination mean?**
**Answer:** The driver on the broken-line side may pass; the driver on the solid-line side may not.

**Q50. What do diamond pavement markings indicate?**
**Answer:** A lane reserved for specific vehicles, potentially with time or vehicle restrictions.

**Q51. What is a two-way left-turn lane?**
**Answer:** A shared centre lane reserved for left-turning traffic travelling in either direction.

**Q52. What does an arrow painted on a lane indicate?**
**Answer:** The vehicle movement(s) permitted in that lane.

**Q53. What is a painted island?**
**Answer:** An area not intended for travel; motorists may cross it to enter or leave a driveway, alleyway, or private drive, but should not park or stop there.

**Q54. What do crosswalk lines mark?**
**Answer:** Pedestrian crosswalks.

**Q55. Are crosswalks present at intersections even when unmarked?**
**Answer:** Yes, unless a sign indicates no crossing.

**Q56. What do stop lines indicate?**
**Answer:** Where vehicles must stop at intersections or railway crossings.

**Q57. Why should drivers not stop in a no-stopping zone?**
**Answer:** They may interfere with vehicles turning from one roadway to another, especially large vehicles needing extra room.

**Q58. What is a slow-moving vehicle sign used for?**
**Answer:** Vehicles travelling less than 40 km/h, such as farm tractors, machinery, or construction equipment.

**Q59. What does an oversized-load sign warn about?**
**Answer:** A wide load is being transported; use caution when passing.

**Q60. What must drivers do in construction zones?**
**Answer:** Obey the posted speed at all times, follow flag-person directions, and be prepared to slow or stop.

**Q61. Does the construction-zone speed limit apply when workers/equipment are not present?**
**Answer:** Yes.

**Q62. What happens to fines when workers are present in a construction zone?**
**Answer:** Fines for speeding are doubled.

**Q63. What is the purpose of warning signs?**
**Answer:** To give advance notice of required actions or potentially hazardous road conditions.

## 3. Basics of Driving (pp. 44–56)

**Q1. What should you do before entering your vehicle?**
**Answer:** Walk around it to check for children, pedestrians, objects in the path, tire condition/inflation, fluid leaks, and other problems.

**Q2. In what direction should you walk around a vehicle parked on a roadway?**
**Answer:** So that you can see traffic coming toward you in the adjacent lane.

**Q3. What is the recommended pre-drive habit order?**
**Answer:** Lock doors; adjust seat/seat back; adjust head restraint; adjust mirrors; fasten seat belt.

**Q4. Why lock vehicle doors?**
**Answer:** To prevent unwanted entry and reduce the chance of doors opening in a collision.

**Q5. How far should you be from the steering wheel?**
**Answer:** At least 25 cm (10 inches), as recommended by Transport Canada.

**Q6. What steering-wheel hand positions are recommended?**
**Answer:** About 9 o'clock for the left hand and 3 o'clock for the right hand.

**Q7. How should elbows be positioned?**
**Answer:** Slightly bent when hands are properly positioned.

**Q8. How should a head restraint be positioned?**
**Answer:** Its centre should be level with the top of the ears; if it tilts, move it forward to reduce the gap, ideally to less than 10 cm/4 inches.

**Q9. How should the inside mirror be adjusted?**
**Answer:** To show as much behind the vehicle as possible, with the rear window framed in the mirror.

**Q10. How should outside mirrors be adjusted?**
**Answer:** To reduce blind spots, with a small portion of the vehicle visible in the nearest side of the mirror.

**Q11. What should you check before moving?**
**Answer:** Clear path, inside and outside mirrors, and blind spots with a shoulder check.

**Q12. What signal is used when moving away from the right side of the road?**
**Answer:** Left turn signal.

**Q13. What signal is used when moving into traffic from the left side of a one-way road?**
**Answer:** Right turn signal.

**Q14. Where should the lap belt sit?**
**Answer:** Low and snug over the hips.

**Q15. Where should the shoulder belt sit?**
**Answer:** Across the chest and shoulder.

**Q16. May the shoulder belt be placed behind the back or under the arm?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q17. Must a seat belt be worn in a vehicle equipped with airbags?**
**Answer:** Yes.

**Q18. What is Park used for in an automatic transmission?**
**Answer:** Starting the engine and leaving the vehicle parked; it locks the transmission.

**Q19. What is Reverse used for?**
**Answer:** Backing or reversing; white/rear clear reverse lights illuminate.

**Q20. What is Neutral used for?**
**Answer:** No gear is selected, wheels are not locked, and there is no power to the wheels; it is used for towing and the engine can start in Neutral.

**Q21. What is Drive used for?**
**Answer:** Normal forward driving.

**Q22. What are lower gears such as First/Second/Third used for?**
**Answer:** More power but less speed, including hills and conditions such as slush, loose gravel, snow, sand, or ice.

**Q23. What is Overdrive used for?**
**Answer:** Higher-speed driving and fuel saving, if equipped.

**Q24. What is a manual transmission clutch used for?**
**Answer:** To connect/disconnect engine power while selecting gears.

**Q25. What is engine lugging?**
**Answer:** The engine moves roughly/bumpily because the selected gear is too high for the conditions.

**Q26. What is engine racing?**
**Answer:** The engine revs without effectively moving the vehicle.

**Q27. When must signal lights be used?**
**Answer:** When moving away from the curb/parking lane, turning left or right, and changing lanes.

**Q28. Why should signals not be used too early or too late?**
**Answer:** To avoid confusing other drivers.

**Q29. What should you do if a turn signal does not cancel automatically?**
**Answer:** Turn it off.

**Q30. How should you accelerate?**
**Answer:** Gradually and smoothly, adjusting to weather, road, and traffic conditions.

**Q31. Should a driver ever exceed the posted speed limit?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q32. Why avoid rapid acceleration?**
**Answer:** It can cause tire spin, increase fuel use, and increase collision risk.

**Q33. What are the three components of total stopping distance?**
**Answer:** Perception distance + reaction distance + braking distance.

**Q34. What is perception time?**
**Answer:** The time needed to recognize a situation and understand that stopping is needed; the guide gives about three-quarters of a second.

**Q35. What is reaction time?**
**Answer:** The time needed to move the foot from accelerator to brake; the average is about three-quarters of a second.

**Q36. What is braking time?**
**Answer:** The time required for the vehicle to stop after the brakes are applied.

**Q37. Name factors that affect stopping distance.**
**Answer:** Visual search, decision-making, alertness/fatigue, alcohol/drugs, vehicle speed, and brake/tire condition, plus road/weather conditions.

**Q38. What is threshold braking?**
**Answer:** Applying the brakes without locking the wheels, maximizing braking efficiency while retaining steering ability.

**Q39. How should reversing be done?**
**Answer:** At a crawl or slow walking speed, only after checking that the path is clear.

**Q40. What is illegal when reversing?**
**Answer:** Reversing into an intersection or crosswalk.

**Q41. When reversing out of a driveway, where should you go?**
**Answer:** Into the nearest traffic lane, then proceed forward; do not reverse into a second traffic lane.

**Q42. When reversing right or left, which way should the steering wheel be turned?**
**Answer:** In the direction you want the rear of the vehicle to go.

**Q43. What should you do when leaving a parallel parking space?**
**Answer:** Check mirrors and blind spot, signal, move out slowly, and watch for traffic/cyclists.

**Q44. How should you enter a parallel-parked vehicle on a two-way street?**
**Answer:** Approach from the front, check traffic, open the door only as much as necessary, and close it quickly.

**Q45. How far should the vehicle be from the rear of a parked vehicle when approaching an angle parking stall?**
**Answer:** About 1.5 metres (5 feet).

**Q46. What is a perpendicular parking stall?**
**Answer:** A stall at a 90-degree angle to the curb.

**Q47. How far should you keep from the rear of the vehicle beside a perpendicular stall?**
**Answer:** About 2 metres (6 feet).

**Q48. How close to the curb should a vehicle be when completing the described angle parking manoeuvre?**
**Answer:** The front wheel should lightly contact the curb or be within 50 cm (20 inches).

**Q49. Which way should wheels point when parked uphill beside a curb on the right side of the road?**
**Answer:** Left, toward the centre of the road.

**Q50. Which way should wheels point when parked uphill without a curb?**
**Answer:** Right, toward the edge of the road.

**Q51. Which way should wheels point when parked downhill?**
**Answer:** Right.

**Q52. What should always be applied when parking on a hill?**
**Answer:** The park brake; automatic transmission in Park or manual transmission in low gear.

**Q53. May you park on a sidewalk or boulevard?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q54. May you park on a crosswalk?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q55. How close may you park to a garage/private roadway/driveway access?**
**Answer:** Not within 1.5 metres (5 feet).

**Q56. How close may you park to a stop or yield sign?**
**Answer:** Not within 5 metres (16 feet).

**Q57. How close may you park to a marked crosswalk?**
**Answer:** Not within 5 metres of the nearest side.

**Q58. How close may you park to a fire hydrant?**
**Answer:** Not within 5 metres.

**Q59. Can you double-park beside another vehicle?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q60. Can you park near an intersection?**
**Answer:** Not closer than 5 metres to the edge of the intersecting roadway unless parking is indicated as permitted.

**Q61. Can you park at an incident if it obstructs traffic or emergency personnel?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q62. What special rule applies to parking on a primary highway outside an urban area?**
**Answer:** Do not park on the roadway, parking lane, or shoulder except where the vehicle is disabled, an emergency exists, or parking is otherwise permitted by law; there must also be clear passage and visibility for 60 m in both directions.

**Q63. Can a vehicle park in a disability-reserved stall without the required identifying placard or plate?**
**Answer:** No.

## 4. Intersections and Turns (pp. 58–69)

**Q1. What is right-of-way?**
**Answer:** The rules that determine who must yield and who may proceed.

**Q2. Does having right-of-way mean you can ignore collision prevention?**
**Answer:** No. Even with right-of-way, you must do everything possible to prevent a collision.

**Q3. What is an intersection?**
**Answer:** A place where two or more roadways meet, creating possible conflicts between vehicles and pedestrians.

**Q4. Who has priority over traffic signs/signals in an emergency direction?**
**Answer:** Directions from a peace/police officer must be followed over traffic signs or signals.

**Q5. Where do you stop at a stop line?**
**Answer:** Before the marked stop line.

**Q6. Where do you stop when there is a marked crosswalk but no stop line?**
**Answer:** Before the marked crosswalk.

**Q7. Where do you stop if there is neither a stop line nor marked crosswalk?**
**Answer:** Within 3 metres (10 feet) of the intersecting roadway, where pedestrians are not interfered with.

**Q8. What is a courtesy corner?**
**Answer:** A three- or four-way stop where all approaches have stop signs.

**Q9. At a four-way stop, who normally proceeds first?**
**Answer:** The vehicle that arrived first.

**Q10. At a four-way stop, if vehicles arrive at the same time, who proceeds first?**
**Answer:** The vehicle on the right.

**Q11. What does a yield sign require?**
**Answer:** Slow down and be prepared to stop; yield to traffic/pedestrians with priority and proceed only when safe.

**Q12. Must you always make a complete stop at a yield sign?**
**Answer:** No; if there is no traffic or pedestrian to which you must yield, you may proceed without a complete stop.

**Q13. At an uncontrolled intersection, whom must you yield to?**
**Answer:** A vehicle on your right.

**Q14. What is the special issue at an uncontrolled T intersection?**
**Answer:** The vehicle on the right has right-of-way even if another driver incorrectly assumes the through road has priority.

**Q15. What must you do when leaving a service road, alley, parking lot, or driveway?**
**Answer:** Stop before any sidewalk crossing, stop before entering the main street, and yield to pedestrians.

**Q16. Can you pass a vehicle stopped for a pedestrian at an intersection?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q17. Should you enter an intersection if traffic prevents you from clearing it?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q18. What direction does traffic travel around a traffic circle/roundabout?**
**Answer:** Counterclockwise.

**Q19. Who must yield when entering a one-lane circular intersection?**
**Answer:** Entering drivers yield to vehicles already in the circle.

**Q20. When should you activate the right signal when exiting a circular intersection?**
**Answer:** After passing the exit immediately before the intended exit.

**Q21. What signal is used when leaving the first available exit of a roundabout?**
**Answer:** Right signal before exiting, following the booklet's roundabout procedure.

**Q22. What is the key difference between a traffic circle and a roundabout?**
**Answer:** A roundabout generally has a smaller centre island, tighter curve and may have a sloped curb for larger commercial vehicles.

**Q23. When turning left at an intersection, whom must you yield to?**
**Answer:** Oncoming traffic and pedestrians as applicable.

**Q24. What should you do before turning left across an approaching vehicle's path?**
**Answer:** Wait until it is safe.

**Q25. What is a turning lane?**
**Answer:** A lane provided for vehicles preparing to make a particular turn.

**Q26. What should you do before changing lanes?**
**Answer:** Signal, check mirrors and blind spot, ensure the gap is safe, and move without interfering with other traffic.

**Q27. What is a U-turn?**
**Answer:** A turn that reverses the direction of travel.

**Q28. When is a U-turn prohibited or unsafe?**
**Answer:** Where signs prohibit it or where it cannot be made safely/legal; follow the roadway rules and signs.

## 5. Highways and Freeways (pp. 70–76)

**Q1. Where should you position your vehicle in a marked lane?**
**Answer:** In the centre of the lane.

**Q2. How should the inside rear-view mirror be positioned?**
**Answer:** To show as much rear-window view as possible.

**Q3. How should outside mirrors be positioned?**
**Answer:** To reduce blind spots while showing a small portion of the vehicle and the area behind in the adjacent lane.

**Q4. Why plan lane changes early?**
**Answer:** To avoid last-minute and dangerous lane changes.

**Q5. What is a blind spot?**
**Answer:** An area around the vehicle that cannot be seen directly through the properly adjusted mirrors.

**Q6. What should you do before passing?**
**Answer:** Check that passing is permitted, the path is clear, there is enough distance/time, and it can be completed safely.

**Q7. What should you do on a two-lane highway before passing?**
**Answer:** Ensure the opposing lane is clear far enough ahead and that markings/signs permit passing.

**Q8. What is an emergency stopping lane?**
**Answer:** The lane bordering the travel lanes on a primary highway for emergencies/stopping.

**Q9. May you drive in the emergency stopping lane to pass?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q10. May you use the emergency stopping lane to let another vehicle pass?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q11. What is merging?**
**Answer:** When two roadways join into one and traffic must cooperate to allow vehicles from the merging lane to enter.

**Q12. Who has right-of-way during a merge?**
**Answer:** Neither side has sole right-of-way; merging is a shared responsibility.

**Q13. Should you stop or abruptly slow when merging?**
**Answer:** No, unless required for safety; the acceleration lane is intended to help reach the highway speed.

**Q14. When should you signal for a merge?**
**Answer:** Before or when entering the acceleration lane.

**Q15. What speed should you aim for when entering a highway?**
**Answer:** Accelerate to the speed of traffic on the main road, consistent with safety and the posted limit.

**Q16. When may you move from the acceleration lane into the highway?**
**Answer:** When safe and legal, after the solid white line of the acceleration lane.

**Q17. If traffic is merging onto a highway, what can a driver on the main roadway do?**
**Answer:** If safe, move left to leave the right lane clear for merging traffic.

**Q18. How should you prepare to exit a highway?**
**Answer:** Plan ahead, get into the proper lane early, signal in advance, and use the deceleration lane.

**Q19. Should most slowing occur on the main highway or deceleration lane?**
**Answer:** If possible, do most of the slowing in the deceleration lane.

**Q20. What should you do if you miss a highway exit?**
**Answer:** Continue to the next exit and plan how to return; never stop or reverse on the highway, emergency stopping lane, or shoulder.

**Q21. What is a weave zone?**
**Answer:** An area where highway entrance and exit traffic share the same lane.

**Q22. How should you drive in a weave zone?**
**Answer:** Control speed and timing carefully and cooperate with entering/exiting traffic.

**Q23. How should you approach a curve?**
**Answer:** Reduce speed before entering, stay centred, look well ahead/around the curve, and maintain a safe steady speed.

**Q24. Can you pass on a curve on a one-lane-each-direction highway?**
**Answer:** No; the guide states it is unsafe and illegal.

**Q25. What effect can wet or icy roads have on curves?**
**Answer:** They make curves more slippery and increase danger.

**Q26. Why should you move right in your lane near the top of a hill?**
**Answer:** To reduce the risk from an oncoming vehicle that may cross the centre line.

**Q27. Should you pass near the top of a hill on a two-lane highway?**
**Answer:** No, because you cannot see far enough ahead.

**Q28. What happens to stopping time going downhill?**
**Answer:** It increases, especially on a steep hill.

**Q29. What can a manual-transmission driver do on a steep downhill?**
**Answer:** Shift to a lower gear to reduce brake overheating risk.

## 6. Emergency Situations and Challenging Conditions (pp. 78–87)

**Q1. What is the goal of emergency braking?**
**Answer:** Stop as quickly as possible without losing control.

**Q2. How does ABS help during emergency braking?**
**Answer:** It allows steering while braking by cycling brake pressure so wheels remain slightly rolling rather than locked.

**Q3. What should you do during emergency braking with ABS?**
**Answer:** Apply steady, firm pressure; do not pump/release the pedal; look and steer where you want to go.

**Q4. What is threshold braking on a non-ABS vehicle?**
**Answer:** Firm braking just before the wheels lock.

**Q5. What should you do if non-ABS wheels lock?**
**Answer:** Ease off the brake slightly to regain steering control, then brake again without locking the wheels.

**Q6. What causes skids?**
**Answer:** Road surface, vehicle speed, turning, tire condition, and driver actions such as excessive speed, sharp steering, hard braking, or rapid acceleration.

**Q7. What should you do during a general skid?**
**Answer:** Keep off the brake and accelerator, look and steer where you want the front of the vehicle to go, and avoid large steering corrections.

**Q8. What is a power skid?**
**Answer:** A skid caused by accelerating too quickly so the wheels spin.

**Q9. How do you recover from a power skid?**
**Answer:** Take your foot off the gas, make needed steering corrections, avoid over-correction, and use gentle acceleration to continue.

**Q10. What is a cornering skid?**
**Answer:** A sideways slide caused by travelling too quickly while turning.

**Q11. How do you recover from a cornering skid?**
**Answer:** Take your foot off the gas, do not use the brakes, look and steer where you want to go.

**Q12. What should you do after driving off the paved roadway?**
**Answer:** Keep a firm grip, do not immediately steer back, take your foot off the gas, avoid braking or brake gradually, check traffic, then return gradually.

**Q13. What should you do if a vehicle has a mechanical problem requiring a stop?**
**Answer:** Change lanes safely and stop in a safe location if possible; do not stop on a bridge or tunnel.

**Q14. What should you do if a disabled vehicle is in a potentially dangerous location?**
**Answer:** Use hazard lights and, if necessary, have occupants leave and wait in a safe location away from the vehicle.

**Q15. What are useful emergency supplies?**
**Answer:** First-aid kit, reflective triangles, booster cables, non-perishable food, jack/spare tire, flashlight/batteries, tow rope, and headlight bulbs.

**Q16. What should you do if you are first at a collision scene?**
**Answer:** Stop safely away from the collision, assist if possible, protect the scene with hazard lights/reflective triangles, and provide accurate information to emergency services.

**Q17. What information should involved drivers exchange after a collision?**
**Answer:** Names, contact information, and insurance details.

**Q18. Should drivers discuss who was at fault at the collision scene?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q19. What witness information should be recorded?**
**Answer:** Names and contact information.

**Q20. When must a collision be reported to police/law enforcement?**
**Answer:** If anyone is injured or killed, total damage exceeds the current applicable threshold, or a traffic-control device, parking meter, or public property is damaged.

**Q21. How can you reduce the chance of an animal collision?**
**Answer:** Slow down, scan the roadside, be extra careful at dusk/dawn, watch for eye reflections, and remember animals may travel in groups.

**Q22. What should you do if an animal suddenly appears?**
**Answer:** Brake hard and prepare for evasive action, choosing an escape route away from oncoming traffic.

**Q23. What speed applies when passing stopped emergency vehicles or tow trucks with flashing lights?**
**Answer:** 60 km/h or the posted speed, whichever is lower, in the lane(s) immediately next to the stopped vehicle.

**Q24. Are fines doubled in the emergency-vehicle roadside zone?**
**Answer:** Yes, for speeding.

**Q25. What should you do if you are not in the lane immediately next to stopped emergency vehicles?**
**Answer:** Remain cautious, reduce speed as appropriate, and leave plenty of space.

**Q26. What following distance does the guide state for emergency vehicles with sirens/lights operating?**
**Answer:** At least 150 metres (500 feet).

**Q27. What should you do when an emergency vehicle approaches with a siren?**
**Answer:** Yield, move as closely as safely possible to the right edge on a two-way road or nearest curb on a one-way road, and stop until it passes.

**Q28. How should you treat a vehicle displaying flashing green lights?**
**Answer:** Treat it like an emergency vehicle; the guide notes volunteer firefighters may use flashing green lights in some municipalities.

**Q29. What should you do when a police vehicle with emergency lights is behind you?**
**Answer:** Move to a safe location and stop so the police vehicle can pass or stop safely behind you.

**Q30. Where should you stop on a two-way road for a police vehicle?**
**Answer:** At the right edge of the roadway in a safe location.

**Q31. Where should you stop on a one-way road for a police vehicle?**
**Answer:** At the nearest edge of the roadway in a safe location.

**Q32. What should you do after being stopped by police?**
**Answer:** Remain calm, put an automatic transmission in Park or manual transmission in Neutral, apply the park brake, remain in the vehicle, have licence/registration/insurance ready, and wait for instructions.

**Q33. May you move your vehicle after a police stop?**
**Answer:** Not until instructed by the police officer.

**Q34. What must drivers do around road-maintenance/construction vehicles?**
**Answer:** Obey traffic-control devices and flag-person directions.

**Q35. What happens to speeding fines in construction zones when workers are present?**
**Answer:** They are doubled.

## 7. Responsible Driving (pp. 89–95)

**Q1. What is proactive driving?**
**Answer:** Driving with the aim of anticipating hazards and taking action to reduce, minimize, or avoid danger before it occurs.

**Q2. Should you assume other drivers will drive carefully?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q3. How far ahead should an urban driver scan?**
**Answer:** At least 12–15 seconds ahead.

**Q4. How far ahead should a rural driver scan?**
**Answer:** At least 20–25 seconds ahead.

**Q5. How often should you glance at rear-view mirrors?**
**Answer:** Every 8–12 seconds, about every block in an urban area.

**Q6. What is visual lead time?**
**Answer:** The time/distance ahead that gives you time to respond to hazards.

**Q7. What are fixed hazards?**
**Answer:** Permanent conditions such as curves, hills, hidden driveways, intersections, and merging roadways.

**Q8. What are variable hazards?**
**Answer:** Changing hazards such as school children, pedestrians, left-turning vehicles, icy surfaces, stale green lights, and emergency vehicles.

**Q9. What is a space cushion?**
**Answer:** Enough space around your vehicle to stop safely or steer around a hazard.

**Q10. What should you do if another driver follows too closely?**
**Answer:** If safe, reduce speed enough to encourage them to pass; otherwise increase the space cushion ahead.

**Q11. What should you do when stopped behind another vehicle?**
**Answer:** Leave enough space to move into another lane without reversing and to reduce the risk of a rear-end collision.

**Q12. What is the minimum following time under normal conditions?**
**Answer:** Two seconds.

**Q13. How should following distance change in poor conditions?**
**Answer:** Increase it.

**Q14. How do you check a two-second following distance?**
**Answer:** When the vehicle ahead passes a fixed object, count one-thousand-and-one, one-thousand-and-two; if you reach the object before finishing, you are too close.

**Q15. What following distance is recommended for large vehicles such as motor homes?**
**Answer:** At least four seconds.

**Q16. What should you do if you miss an exit or turn?**
**Answer:** Continue to the next exit/intersection; never reverse on a roadway to return.

**Q17. What should you do when using a cellular phone while driving?**
**Answer:** Do not use it; stop in a safe, legal location if you need to make/receive a call or message.

**Q18. Does the booklet's distraction warning include hands-free cellular phones?**
**Answer:** Yes; it says this also applies to hands-free cellular telephones.

**Q19. Name examples of prohibited/distracting activities listed in the guide.**
**Answer:** Hand-held phone use, texting/e-mailing, using electronic devices, programming portable audio players, entering GPS information, reading printed material, writing/printing/sketching, and personal grooming.

**Q20. Who is responsible for securing passengers under 16?**
**Answer:** The driver.

**Q21. What happens if a passenger aged 16 or older is not properly secured?**
**Answer:** The passenger can be fined.

**Q22. What child requires a child safety seat?**
**Answer:** A child under six years of age and not more than 18 kg (40 lb) must be properly secured in a child safety seat.

**Q23. What Canadian standard label must child safety seats have?**
**Answer:** The label stating the product meets Canada Motor Vehicle Safety Standard 213.

**Q24. Can a child safety seat designed for another country be used?**
**Answer:** The guide says not to use one designed for countries other than Canada.

**Q25. What type of restraint is required from birth?**
**Answer:** A rear-facing infant or convertible seat until the manufacturer-recommended weight for switching forward-facing is reached.

**Q26. What weight range is given for forward-facing child seats?**
**Answer:** 10–18 kg (22–40 lb).

**Q27. What should be used for children over 18 kg (40 lb) before using only a seat belt?**
**Answer:** An approved booster seat.

**Q28. What is UAS/LATCH?**
**Answer:** A universal anchor system used to secure child safety seats in newer vehicles/seats.

**Q29. What fuel-use effect does driving 10 km/h above 90 km/h have according to the guide?**
**Answer:** About 10% more fuel.

**Q30. What can rapid acceleration and speed changes do to fuel use?**
**Answer:** Increase fuel consumption by as much as 40%.

**Q31. How long should you idle before turning off the engine if parked?**
**Answer:** If parking for more than 10 seconds, the guide recommends turning the engine off.

**Q32. How much fuel does an average vehicle use in 10 minutes of idling?**
**Answer:** About one-third of a litre; a larger engine can use up to one-half litre.

**Q33. How early should a block heater be switched on?**
**Answer:** About two hours before driving.

**Q34. How much can proper block-heater use improve overall fuel economy according to the guide?**
**Answer:** As much as 10%.

**Q35. How much can a poorly tuned engine increase fuel consumption?**
**Answer:** Up to 50%.

**Q36. How much can a clogged air filter reduce fuel efficiency?**
**Answer:** About 10%.

**Q37. How much can under-inflated tires increase fuel consumption?**
**Answer:** About 5%, while also increasing tire wear and reducing traction.

**Q38. What can air conditioning do to fuel use in stop-and-go traffic?**
**Answer:** Increase it by about 10–25%.

**Q39. What can air conditioning do to fuel use at highway speeds?**
**Answer:** Increase it by about 3–4%.

**Q40. What should you do when fuelling a vehicle?**
**Answer:** Do not smoke/use flames, do not use a cellular phone, turn the ignition off, keep children from the nozzle, do not prop the nozzle open, stay by the nozzle, do not overfill, and place portable fuel containers on the ground when filling.

## 8. Sharing the Road (pp. 97–105)

**Q1. Who are vulnerable road users?**
**Answer:** Pedestrians, cyclists, and motorcycle riders.

**Q2. What should you do at a yellow flashing pedestrian-activated light?**
**Answer:** Slow to 30 km/h and yield to pedestrians wanting to cross.

**Q3. Should you pass another vehicle approaching a crosswalk?**
**Answer:** No; the other vehicle may be slowing or stopping for a pedestrian.

**Q4. Are all crosswalks marked?**
**Answer:** No; the guide says crosswalk rules apply at all intersections.

**Q5. How should you treat a visually impaired pedestrian?**
**Answer:** Be considerate and watch for a white cane or guide dog.

**Q6. What does not overdriving your headlights mean?**
**Answer:** Drive so you can stop within the distance clearly visible in your headlights.

**Q7. Should you share a lane with a motorcycle?**
**Answer:** No. A motorcycle rider needs the whole lane.

**Q8. Why might a motorcycle rider move within the lane?**
**Answer:** To avoid hazards such as potholes and maintain a space cushion.

**Q9. How much following space should you give a motorcycle?**
**Answer:** Extra space because motorcycles can stop quickly.

**Q10. What conditions can increase risk for motorcycles?**
**Answer:** Poor weather, road conditions, and road hazards.

**Q11. What should you do when following a large vehicle?**
**Answer:** Increase following time/distance and maintain a larger viewing area.

**Q12. Why should you not move into the space directly in front of a large vehicle near a traffic light?**
**Answer:** The large vehicle needs extra stopping distance if the light changes.

**Q13. How can large vehicles affect your vehicle when passing?**
**Answer:** They can create strong wind gusts, especially with crosswinds.

**Q14. How can you tell you are too close behind a large vehicle?**
**Answer:** If you cannot see both of its side mirrors, you are too close.

**Q15. What should you do when passing a large vehicle?**
**Answer:** Allow more time and distance in the oncoming lane and do not pass unless the pass can be completed safely.

**Q16. After passing a heavy commercial vehicle, how much space should you leave before returning to its lane?**
**Answer:** Extra space because it needs more distance to slow or stop.

**Q17. Why should you stay away from the right side of a large vehicle turning right?**
**Answer:** You could be squeezed between the vehicle and the curb/edge.

**Q18. What should you do if a large vehicle needs to cross the centre line or part of your lane to turn?**
**Answer:** Stop back from the intersection and allow it room to complete the turn.

**Q19. How long may logs extend from the rear of a log-hauling truck?**
**Answer:** Up to about 9 metres (30 feet).

**Q20. Should you pass a log-hauling truck while it is turning?**
**Answer:** No.

**Q21. What is special about oversized-load convoys?**
**Answer:** They may use pilot vehicles and travel slowly; passing requires enough room and sight distance.

**Q22. What do alternating amber school-bus lights mean?**
**Answer:** A warning that the bus is preparing to stop; be ready to stop.

**Q23. On an undivided two-lane highway, which directions must stop for a school bus with alternating red lights?**
**Answer:** Traffic from both directions.

**Q24. On a four-lane undivided highway, which lanes must stop for a school bus?**
**Answer:** All four lanes.

**Q25. On a divided highway with a median, who must stop for the school bus?**
**Answer:** Vehicles approaching from the rear of the bus must stop; vehicles approaching from the front may proceed with caution while watching for pedestrians.

**Q26. How far away should you stop for a school bus displaying alternating red lights?**
**Answer:** About 20 metres (four to five car lengths).

**Q27. When may you proceed after stopping for a school bus?**
**Answer:** When the alternating red lights are off and the stop sign on the driver's side is no longer extended.

**Q28. What do snowplow flashing amber and red lights mean?**
**Answer:** Snowplow ahead.

**Q29. Why stay well back from a snowplow?**
**Answer:** To improve reaction time and reduce damage from rocks/sanding material.

**Q30. When is passing a snowplow prohibited?**
**Answer:** When your view is obstructed, passing would impede the snowplow, or passing cannot be done safely.

**Q31. How often may snowplows move aside to let traffic pass?**
**Answer:** Typically every five to eight kilometres where it is safe.

**Q32. What does a railway crossing sign mean?**
**Answer:** Drivers must yield to all trains.

**Q33. How close must you stop when a railway crossing has a stop sign?**
**Answer:** Between 5 metres (15 feet) and 15 metres (50 feet) from the nearest rail.

**Q34. When must you stop for a train near a railway crossing?**
**Answer:** When a train is visible or sounding a signal and approaching within 500 metres of the crossing.

**Q35. What should flashing red railway lights and bells make you do?**
**Answer:** Stop at least 5 metres from the nearest rail and wait until the lights/bells stop and the train has passed or stopped.

**Q36. May you drive around a lowered railway gate?**
**Answer:** Never.

**Q37. What should you do if your vehicle stalls on railway tracks?**
**Answer:** Get everyone out immediately and move away from the tracks; if a train is approaching, move away from the vehicle and tracks, preferably in the direction from which the train is approaching.

**Q38. How far may an LRT train take to stop in an emergency?**
**Answer:** Up to about 100 metres (330 feet).

**Q39. Should you drive around LRT gates after a train passes?**
**Answer:** No; another train may approach from the other direction.

**Q40. What must funeral-procession participants do at a stop sign or red light?**
**Answer:** They may continue through without stopping if closely following the procession and it is safe, with headlights on.

**Q41. May you pass through a funeral procession?**
**Answer:** No; yield until it has passed.

**Q42. What should you remember about off-highway vehicles?**
**Answer:** Watch for ATVs, snowmobiles, and similar vehicles near or crossing highways.

## 9. Driving Within the Law (pp. 107–118)

**Q1. What factors can impair fitness to drive?**
**Answer:** Alcohol, drugs, medications, stress, fatigue, lack of sleep, illness, emotional strain, boredom, long driving periods, physical activity, and eye strain.

**Q2. How does fatigue affect driving?**
**Answer:** It increases reaction time and can impair judgement and decision-making.

**Q3. What should you do if tired while driving?**
**Answer:** Stop for a rest and walk around the vehicle.

**Q4. Can prescription or over-the-counter medication impair driving?**
**Answer:** Yes; side effects may include drowsiness or dizziness.

**Q5. What should you know before driving after taking medication?**
**Answer:** Its effects and possible interactions with alcohol or drugs; consult a doctor/pharmacist as appropriate.

**Q6. When does impairment begin according to the guide?**
**Answer:** With the first drink or drug use.

**Q7. What are possible consequences of impaired driving?**
**Answer:** Fines, criminal record, jail, licence loss, higher insurance premiums, property damage, injury/death, and employment loss where driving is required.

**Q8. How are demerit points recorded?**
**Answer:** When a driver is convicted of an offence, including by voluntarily paying the ticket, being found guilty in court, or being convicted in absence after failing to appear.

**Q9. When does a fully licensed driver receive a demerit warning letter?**
**Answer:** At 8–14 points within two years.

**Q10. At how many points is a fully licensed driver automatically suspended?**
**Answer:** 15 or more within two years.

**Q11. How many points remain after a fully licensed driver's suspension?**
**Answer:** Seven.

**Q12. When is a GDL driver warned about demerit points?**
**Answer:** At 4–7 points within two years.

**Q13. At how many points is a GDL driver automatically suspended?**
**Answer:** Eight or more within two years.

**Q14. How many points remain after a GDL suspension?**
**Answer:** Three.

**Q15. After how long are demerit points from a conviction removed?**
**Answer:** Two years from the conviction date.

**Q16. How many points are assigned for failing to remain at a collision scene?**
**Answer:** 7.

**Q17. How many points for careless driving?**
**Answer:** 6.

**Q18. How many points for failing to stop for a school bus?**
**Answer:** 6.

**Q19. How many points for racing?**
**Answer:** 6.

**Q20. How many points for speeding 51 km/h or more over the limit?**
**Answer:** 6.

**Q21. How many points for failing to stop at a railway crossing involving a school bus or certain dangerous-goods vehicle?**
**Answer:** 5.

**Q22. How many points for failing to stop for a peace officer?**
**Answer:** 5.

**Q23. How many points for failing to yield right-of-way to a pedestrian in a crosswalk?**
**Answer:** 4.

**Q24. How many points for following too closely?**
**Answer:** 4.

**Q25. How many points for speeding 31–50 km/h over the limit?**
**Answer:** 4.

**Q26. How many points for failing to stop at a stop sign?**
**Answer:** 3.

**Q27. How many points for failing to stop at a red light?**
**Answer:** 3.

**Q28. How many points for improper passing?**
**Answer:** 3.

**Q29. How many points for stunting?**
**Answer:** 3.

**Q30. How many points for speeding 16–30 km/h over the limit?**
**Answer:** 3.

**Q31. How many points for failing to obey a traffic-control device?**
**Answer:** 2.

**Q32. How many points for improper backing into an intersection/crosswalk or unsafe backing onto a highway?**
**Answer:** 2.

**Q33. How many points for improper turns or an improper U-turn?**
**Answer:** 2.

**Q34. How many points for a traffic-lane violation?**
**Answer:** 2.

**Q35. How many points for speeding up to 15 km/h over the limit?**
**Answer:** 2.

**Q36. What GDL-specific offence can earn 2 points for a Class 7 learner?**
**Answer:** Driving between midnight and 5 a.m.

**Q37. What other GDL-specific offences can earn 2 points?**
**Answer:** Having more passengers than seat belts and having an unqualified accompanying supervisor.

**Q38. What are the automatic suspension terms listed for fully licensed/GDL drivers?**
**Answer:** First: one month; second within one year: three months; subsequent terms are governed by the applicable rules in the guide.

**Q39. What federal law covers criminal impaired-driving offences?**
**Answer:** The Criminal Code of Canada.

**Q40. What is Alberta's Immediate Roadside Sanctions program?**
**Answer:** A provincial impaired-driving program using escalating roadside penalties.

**Q41. What does IRS ZERO: Novice apply to?**
**Answer:** All alcohol and/or drugs for Class 7 learner and Class 5-GDL drivers; it is zero tolerance.

**Q42. What is the first listed IRS ZERO: Novice consequence?**
**Answer:** Immediate 30-day driver licence suspension, 7-day vehicle seizure, and a $200 fine plus 20% victim fine surcharge.

**Q43. What does IRS ZERO: Commercial apply to?**
**Answer:** Commercial drivers operating a commercial vehicle in a commercial capacity; it uses zero tolerance for alcohol/drugs.

**Q44. What does IRS: WARN apply to?**
**Answer:** When an officer has reasonable grounds to believe a driver operated a vehicle with BAC at or above 50 mg alcohol per 100 mL blood.

**Q45. What is the first IRS: WARN occurrence penalty listed?**
**Answer:** Three-day immediate licence suspension, three-day vehicle seizure, and $300 fine plus 20% victim fine surcharge.

**Q46. What does IRS: FAIL address?**
**Answer:** Impaired driving by alcohol/drugs, prescribed blood thresholds within two hours after driving, combined alcohol/drug thresholds, or refusal to comply with a lawful demand without reasonable excuse.

**Q47. What is the first listed IRS: FAIL fixed-term suspension structure?**
**Answer:** 90 days with no driving under any circumstances, followed by a further 12-month suspension, during which participation in the applicable interlock program may permit restricted driving.

**Q48. What is SafeRoads Alberta?**
**Answer:** The administrative adjudication branch responsible for reviews of provincial administrative penalties for impaired drivers and vehicle seizures.

**Q49. What is the Registrar Reconsideration Process?**
**Answer:** A process for reviewing non-impaired driving decisions, including certain administrative matters and Ignition Interlock Program exemptions.

**Q50. What must a driver do during a suspension?**
**Answer:** Serve the suspension and comply with all reinstatement conditions; operating while suspended is a serious offence.

**Q51. What happens when reinstatement requirements are completed?**
**Answer:** The driver goes to an Alberta registry agent with proof of compliance, and the licence can be issued/reinstated according to the process.

**Q52. What is an ignition interlock?**
**Answer:** A device that measures breath alcohol concentration and prevents the vehicle from starting when the reading exceeds the specified threshold.

## 10. Towing a Trailer (pp. 120–122)

**Q1. What must you have before towing a trailer on Alberta roads?**
**Answer:** Valid Alberta trailer registration and a clearly visible valid licence plate.

**Q2. What trailer lighting is required?**
**Answer:** Working tail lights, brake lights, and turn signals activated from the towing vehicle.

**Q3. When are safety chains required?**
**Answer:** With a ball-and-socket hitch; chains must be crossed under the trailer tongue and securely fastened, except for fifth-wheel trailers.

**Q4. What should be done with a trailer's emergency breakaway system?**
**Answer:** Connect it properly according to the manufacturer's instructions.

**Q5. When are extension mirrors required?**
**Answer:** When the trailer is wider than the towing vehicle.

**Q6. When are trailer brakes not required according to the booklet?**
**Answer:** For trailers with a gross weight of 910 kg (2,000 lb) or less, or trailers weighing less than half the unloaded weight of the towing vehicle.

**Q7. How should you drive when towing a trailer?**
**Answer:** Allow extra time/distance, maintain a safe following distance, accelerate and brake more slowly, allow extra room for merging/changing lanes, and plan ahead.

**Q8. Which lane should slower vehicles use on a multi-lane highway?**
**Answer:** The right lane.

**Q9. What should you do if traffic builds behind you while towing?**
**Answer:** Find a safe place off the road and allow others to pass.

**Q10. What is off-tracking?**
**Answer:** The trailer wheels follow a path inside the path of the towing vehicle's wheels during a turn.

**Q11. How should you account for off-tracking when turning left with a trailer?**
**Answer:** Allow room for the trailer wheels by steering appropriately and checking the trailer wheels in the left outside mirror.

**Q12. What should you do when turning right with a trailer?**
**Answer:** Use the reverse of the left-turn procedure and allow for off-tracking so the trailer does not hit the curb, parked vehicle, or pedestrian.

**Q13. What is recommended when backing a trailer?**
**Answer:** Have someone guide you if possible; otherwise walk to the rear and ensure the path is clear.

**Q14. How fast should you back a trailer?**
**Answer:** No faster than walking pace.

**Q15. When beginning to back a trailer left, which way do you turn the steering wheel?**
**Answer:** Right; to make the trailer go left.

**Q16. When beginning to back a trailer right, which way do you turn the steering wheel?**
**Answer:** Left; to make the trailer go right.

**Q17. How much should you initially turn the steering wheel when backing a trailer?**
**Answer:** About one-quarter to one-half turn, then return toward normal as the trailer begins to turn.

**Q18. Why are small steering movements recommended while backing a trailer?**
**Answer:** They provide better control and reduce over-correction.

**Q19. What is a special visibility problem when backing a trailer to the right?**
**Answer:** Blind spots behind the trailer limit visibility.
